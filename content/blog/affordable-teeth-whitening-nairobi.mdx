## Why professional whitening is good value

Premium care at an affordable price is what we aim for, and whitening is a good example of where paying a little more up front often saves money and discomfort later.

### Your teeth are checked first

Not every dark or yellow tooth is simply stained. Discolouration can come from decay, an old filling, a tooth that has been knocked, or worn enamel. Whitening gel does not change the colour of fillings, crowns or veneers either. A quick examination means you do not spend money on a treatment that cannot fix the real problem.

### Trays that actually fit

Our take-home kits use **custom trays** shaped to your teeth. A good fit keeps the whitening gel on the teeth, where it belongs, and away from your gums. One-size-fits-all trays tend to be loose, which can mean uneven results and gel spreading where it should not.

### Guidance on sensitivity

Some people notice temporary sensitivity during or after whitening. With professional treatment you have someone to ask, and we can advise on how to manage it. That support is part of what you pay for.

## DIY whitening: what people try, and the risks

Home remedies and shop-bought products are tempting because they cost less. Here is a fair look at the most common ones.

### Charcoal powders and toothpastes

Charcoal products are often marketed as natural whiteners. They work mainly by scrubbing, and abrasive products can wear away enamel over time. Enamel does not grow back, and as it thins, the yellower layer underneath can show through more, which is the opposite of what you want.

### Whitening strips

Over-the-counter strips can lift some surface staining. Because they are not made for your mouth, they may not cover every tooth evenly, which can lead to patchy results. Used too often or for too long, they can also irritate gums or increase sensitivity.

### Baking soda, lemon and other kitchen remedies

Baking soda is abrasive, and acidic foods such as lemon or vinegar can soften enamel. Rubbing an acid and an abrasive together on your teeth is a combination best avoided. These remedies may make teeth feel cleaner for a moment, but they carry a real risk of long-term damage.

## Comparing the true cost

When you compare options, look beyond the price on the box:

- **Results**: professional whitening is planned around your teeth, so results are more even and predictable
- **Safety**: gums and enamel are protected, and any underlying problem is spotted first
- **Lifespan**: with custom trays, you can top up at home instead of starting from scratch
- **Hidden costs**: repairing worn enamel or treating sensitivity can cost more than doing it properly the first time

A cheap product that has to be repeated again and again, or that harms your enamel, is rarely the affordable choice in the end.

## Making your results last

Whatever option you choose, a few habits help keep your smile bright:

- Rinse with water after chai, coffee or other dark drinks
- Brush twice a day and clean between your teeth
- Avoid smoking, which stains teeth quickly
- Keep up routine cleanings, which remove surface stains

## Frequently asked questions

### Which whitening option is the most affordable?

The custom take-home tray kit has the lowest starting price, from KSh 15,000. It whitens gradually at home and the trays can be reused for top-ups.

### Does whitening work on crowns, veneers or fillings?

No. Whitening gel works on natural tooth enamel and does not change the colour of crowns, veneers or fillings. We will check for these at your consultation and talk you through your options.

### Will whitening make my teeth sensitive?

Some people feel temporary sensitivity, which usually settles. We will explain what to expect and how to keep it to a minimum.

### Is whitening suitable for everyone?

Not always. Teeth and gums should be healthy first, and some types of discolouration respond better to other treatments. A free consultation is the simplest way to find out.
