## What a crown does

A crown is a cap that covers the whole visible part of a tooth. It restores the tooth's shape and strength and protects it from further damage. Your dentist may suggest a crown when a tooth is:

- Cracked, broken or badly worn
- Weakened by a very large filling
- Badly discoloured or misshapen, where a filling or bonding would not be enough
- Supporting a bridge to replace a missing tooth
- Being restored on top of a dental implant

A crown is usually made after the tooth has been shaped to make room for it. Local anaesthetic numbs the area, so most patients feel pressure rather than pain.

## The three main crown materials

### Tooth-coloured crowns

These are made to match the shade of your natural teeth, so they blend in with your smile. They are a popular choice for front teeth and anywhere that shows when you talk or laugh.

**Pros**
- Natural appearance that is matched to your surrounding teeth
- No metal visible at the gum line
- A good option when appearance matters most

**Cons**
- Can be less forgiving than metal for people who grind their teeth heavily
- May need a little more of the tooth to be shaped, depending on the case

### Silver crowns

Silver-coloured metal crowns are strong and practical. They are often used on back teeth, where chewing forces are greatest and appearance matters less.

**Pros**
- Durable and able to cope well with chewing forces
- Often a cost-effective choice for back teeth
- Usually need less tooth to be removed than some tooth-coloured options

**Cons**
- Clearly visible, so rarely chosen for front teeth
- Some people simply prefer a tooth-coloured look

### Gold crowns

Gold crowns have a long history in dentistry. Gold is a gentle, workable metal that fits closely to the tooth and is kind to the opposing teeth it bites against.

**Pros**
- Strong and long-lasting with good care
- Gentle on the teeth they bite against
- Close, precise fit

**Cons**
- The colour is very noticeable
- Generally a higher-cost option because of the material

Your dentist will recommend what suits your tooth, your bite and your budget. Often, the best answer is a mix: tooth-coloured crowns where they show, and a strong metal crown at the back.

## What affects the cost of a crown

When patients ask about dental crown cost in Nairobi, the honest answer is that a few factors come together:

- **Material**: tooth-coloured, silver and gold crowns differ in material and making costs
- **Which tooth**: front and back teeth have different demands on looks and strength
- **The tooth's condition**: decay may need treating, or a weak tooth may need building up, before the crown is fitted
- **Number of crowns**: several crowns, or a crown and bridge, form a bigger treatment plan
- **Visits needed**: shaping, temporary protection and fitting the final crown can take more than one appointment

## Is a crown always the right answer?

Not necessarily. Smaller damage can often be repaired more conservatively with a filling or bonding, which keeps more of your natural tooth. ## Caring for your crown

Crowns are strong, but the tooth underneath can still decay at the edges. To help your crown last:

- Brush twice a day and clean between your teeth
- Avoid using your teeth to open bottles or packets
- Ask about a mouth guard if you grind your teeth at night
- Keep up regular check-ups so the edges of the crown can be checked

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a dental crown cost in Nairobi?

It depends on the material, the tooth and any preparation needed. An accurate quote needs an examination, and your free consultation gives you a clear cost before treatment starts.

### Which crown material lasts longest?

All three can last many years with good care. Metal crowns are especially hard-wearing on back teeth, while tooth-coloured crowns are chosen where appearance matters. Your bite and habits make a big difference.

### Can I have a tooth-coloured crown on a back tooth?

Often, yes. Your dentist will check your bite and whether you grind your teeth before recommending the best material.

### Does getting a crown hurt?

Local anaesthetic numbs the area, so most patients feel pressure rather than pain. Some mild sensitivity afterwards is common and usually settles.
